## Abstract
**Background.** Repeat families, satellite arrays and multigene families can contain tens of thousands to millions of related copies, too many to inspect and too diverged for one identity threshold. Clustering tools return real copies that carry their own mutations, or consensuses whose quality depends on where the threshold fell; methods that define subfamilies statistically need every copy aligned to one reference and set aside truncated copies.
**Results.** SubFam orders the copies along a k-mer guide tree, cuts the order into chunks of N neighbours, calls a plurality consensus for each chunk and aligns the consensuses. The result is a short alignment that can be read by eye, plus a table of which copies built each row. No reference and no identity threshold are needed; N sets the resolution in copies. Rows are faithful to their copies in young and middle families and less so in old ones. A run on 100,000 copies took 30 minutes on four cores. ## Background
Interspersed repeats, satellite arrays and multigene families can contain tens of thousands to millions of related copies. They are too many to inspect and too diverged to group by one identity threshold: a threshold that separates the subfamilies of a young family splits an old one into singletons, and one that suits an old family merges the subfamilies of a young one. Identity-based clustering (CD-HIT [6], VSEARCH [5], MMseqs2 [7]) returns either a real copy, which carries all of its own private mutations, or a cluster consensus whose quality depends on where the threshold fell. Methods that define subfamilies statistically, from the whole-genome Alu analysis of Price et al. [1] to its successor COSEG, require every copy to be aligned to one reference over a shared window, and so set aside truncated or rearranged copies; the Dfam curation guidelines [2] recommend visual review of their output and describe COSEG as not appropriate where the structure looks truncated. Annotation pipelines assign each copy the best-scoring library consensus, a label without a stated uncertainty: more than 10 % of replicate Alu pairs are labelled differently [3].
The need that remains is smaller than defining subfamilies: bringing an unorganised multitude into an order that a person can read. SubFam does this and nothing more. It orders copies by k-mer similarity, averages blocks of neighbours into consensuses and aligns the consensuses, so that the variants shared by a group stay visible while the private mutations of single copies are averaged away. A table records which copies built each row. SubFam makes no subfamily calls and does not place individual orphan copies.

## Implementation
**Algorithm.** (1) *Order.* All sequences are placed along a guide tree built from k-mer counts (default k = 6, range 3-12). The distance of two copies is the weighted Jaccard distance of their k-mer counts. The tree is built by UPGMA with the tie rules of the k-mer tree of ViewAlign [10], of which SubFam embeds a numpy port; at each merge the two subtrees are flipped so that their closest ends meet. No alignment is computed in this step. Alternatives are the MAFFT [8] guide tree (`-m`) and MAFFT PartTree (`-P`) for inputs above about 20,000 copies. With `-r`, k-mers are strand-independent and each copy is oriented like its predecessor in the order. (2) *Chunk.* The order is cut into blocks of N consecutive sequences (default 50); the remainder joins the last block, so no sequence is dropped. (3) *Consensus.* Each chunk is aligned with MAFFT, chunks in parallel, and a plurality consensus is called: a base is written when at least p x n of the n sequences agree (default p = 0.36), with the scoring rules of EMBOSS cons [9] (EDNAFULL) reimplemented in awk. Columns without consensus are dropped (`-a` keeps them as N). With `-c` the threshold is p x the number of sequences that span the column (internal gaps counted, end gaps not), with at least three, so that consensuses of truncated copies extend as far as the data does. (4) *Align.* The chunk consensuses are aligned with MAFFT L-INS-i. Outputs: consensuses, their alignment (FASTA and MSF), and a table of copy, chunk and strand.
**Parameters.** N is the resolution, in copies: a subfamily needs roughly N copies in the input to obtain a row of its own. There is no identity threshold.
**Equivalence checks.** The k-mer order is identical to the JavaScript implementation, and the awk consensus and MSF writer are byte-identical to EMBOSS cons and seqret (3,600 runs for cons) [tests/check_equivalence.sh; reported in the repository, not re-run for this draft].
**Complexity.** The k-mer ordering uses an n x n float32 matrix (2.2 GB at 20,000 copies) and is intended for about 20,000 copies or fewer; PartTree ordering removes this limit. Chunk alignment is linear in n and parallel. The final alignment is quadratic in the number of rows [docs/SCALING.md].
**Simulations.** (a) Simple [benchmark/simulate.py]: a random 300 bp ancestor evolves along a balanced tree to eight subfamilies (four diagnostic substitutions per branch, so sisters differ at eight sites); the subfamilies have 600, 400, 300, 250, 200, 150, 60 and 40 copies; each copy decays independently from its subfamily master by 3, 8 or 15 % (85 % substitutions, 15 % short indels); eight seeds per scenario [benchmark/multiseed]. (b) Harder [benchmark/hard/simulate2.py]: a CpG-rich ancestor with CpG decay at six times the rate of other sites, one to five source elements per subfamily, per-copy ages drawn around the scenario mean, transition bias, and 5'-truncated copies; scenarios young, middle, old, mixed ages (four young and four old subfamilies) and 80 % truncated copies; four seeds. (c) L1-like [benchmark/hard/simulate_line.py]: 6 kb copies, 97 % 5'-truncated, 15 % with an inverted 5' part, 10 % with an internal deletion.
**Scoring.** A subfamily is recovered when an output row whose copies are mostly that subfamily matches the true master at >= 99 % identity and, in the harder simulations, covers >= 95 % of it. Because CpG decay moves every consensus off the master at CpG sites, the harder simulations report identity over all columns and over non-CpG columns. Comparison: VSEARCH `--cluster_fast --consout` at identities 0.95 to 0.65; in the first benchmark also CD-HIT-EST with a consensus per cluster and MMseqs2 centroids [benchmark/results.tsv].

## Results and discussion
**Recovery across family ages without an identity threshold** [benchmark/multiseed]. In the simple simulation SubFam `-n 20` recovered all eight source consensuses in all 24 runs (three ages, eight seeds) with 100 rows for 2,000 copies; `-n 50` (40 rows) averaged 8.0, 7.9 and 7.4 of 8 for the young, middle and old family and lost the smallest subfamilies (40 and 60 copies, below N) in some runs. The identity at which VSEARCH recovers the sources depends on the age of the family: 0.95 or 0.90 for the young family, 0.85 or 0.80 for the middle one, 0.70 for the old one. It needed 92 to 1,278 rows for the young and middle families and about 540 for the old one, where even an identity chosen for each replicate using the truth recovered 5.25 of 8 on average.
**A harder simulation** [benchmark/hard, four seeds]. With CpG decay, source elements, age mixture and truncated copies, SubFam `-n 20` recovered (non-CpG identity) 7.75, 7.5 and 6.25 of 8 for the young, middle and old family, 7.0 in the mixed-age set and 6.5 with 80 % truncated copies (7.25 with `-c`), always with 100 rows; the best fixed VSEARCH identity recovered 7.75, 7.5, 5.5, 6.0 and 4.75 with 50 to 1,000 rows. Identity over all columns was below 99 % for nearly all consensuses of every method, because of CpG decay. `-c` changed nothing at 30 % truncation and helped at 80 %; the MAFFT guide-tree order failed with truncated copies (0.75 of 8) where the k-mer tree did not.
**How well rows stand for their copies** [benchmark/multiseed/fidelity.py, seeds 1-4]. A copy's own row was its best-matching row for 98.1, 87.3 and 57.5 % of the copies of the young, middle and old family (`-n 50`); median identity to the own row was 96.7, 91.0 and 82.6 %, while a copy is nearest its true master in essentially 100 % of cases. Fidelity falls with age because chunks mix sister subfamilies (chunk purity 0.99, 0.91, 0.71). A cohesion statistic per chunk (fraction of members agreeing with the consensus in core columns) separated pure from mixed chunks with an AUC of 0.92 in one simulation [benchmark/hard/chunk_stats.py; prototype].
**Scale** [docs/SCALING.md; not re-run for this draft]. With PartTree ordering and N = 50, 100,000 simulated copies took 30 minutes on four cores (ordering 6 min 14 s, chunk alignments 6 min 12 s, final alignment of 2,000 consensuses 17 min 35 s) and recovered all eight subfamilies at 100 % identity (chunk purity 0.944).
**Truncated long copies (L1-like)** [benchmark/hard/README.md; exploratory]. [UPDATE with all seeds.] Seed 1: chunk purity 0.35-0.46 for every method; with `-c`, five of eight sources were recovered at 20 % coverage of the master against two without it; the MAFFT order recovered none; VSEARCH recovered 0-1 with 570-1,980 rows; discovering rows from the long copies only and placing the others by best hit put 41-56 % of copies in a row of their own subfamily.
**Real Alu data** [PENDING]. Hg38 recovery against Dfam and the 213 consensuses. An illustration is available: of 316 Sanger-sequenced polymorphic Alu loci [4], 15 rows contained the AluY, AluYa5 and AluYb8 consensuses at 1.000 identity, where VSEARCH needed about 140 rows and CD-HIT-EST 125 for 0.99 [benchmark/alu_konkel]; the references are the consensuses that labelled these copies, so this is not a validation.
**Limitations.** Chunks have a fixed size, so an abundant subfamily fills several rows and a chunk can straddle two. Row fidelity falls with family age. N must be chosen for the rarest group of interest. Truncated long copies are ordered poorly by whole-sequence k-mer distance; `-c` lets consensuses extend and the recommended route is to discover rows from the long copies and place fragments afterwards. The simulations are of our own design, not calibrated to a genome, with one family length in the first two sets and no recombination.

## Conclusions
SubFam brings a collection of related sequences that is too large to read into a short alignment that can be read, and keeps the provenance of every row. Without an identity threshold its rows contain the known consensuses of families of any age in simulation, with far fewer rows than identity clustering needs, and it runs on 10^5 copies on a workstation. It does not call subfamilies and does not resolve orphan copies.
